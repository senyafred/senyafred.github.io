I am a PhD candidate in **Applied Mathematics** at the [University of Vermont](https://www.uvm.edu/cems/mathstat), advised by Prof. [Daniel Bentil](https://www.uvm.edu/cems/mathstat). I build and analyze mathematical models of biological systems — turning differential equations, optimization, and data into insight about how living systems behave in space and time.

My research sits at the intersection of **mathematical biology, systems biology, and scientific computing**. Recently I developed an equation-based framework that couples **flux balance analysis** with **reaction–diffusion PDEs** to simulate the spatio-temporal dynamics of microbial communities (first-author preprint, *Senya et al.*, 2026). I also work on predictive modeling of chronic-disease progression, Bayesian inference for experimental data, and agent-based and complex-systems models. Day to day I code in **Python, MATLAB, R, and Mathematica**, with LaTeX for writing.

Alongside research, I am an **Instructor of Mathematics** at UVM, teaching across the curriculum from algebra through calculus, and I care a lot about building inclusive, effective classrooms. Before Vermont I earned an MPhil in Scientific Computing and Industrial Modeling in Ghana and worked in data analysis and education across academia, industry, and the public sector.

I am currently **open to research and data-science opportunities**. Please feel free to [get in touch](mailto:fsenya@uvm.edu), browse my [projects](/projects/), or view my [CV](/cv/).
